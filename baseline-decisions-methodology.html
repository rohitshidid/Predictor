Seven decisions turned last season into an opening table — and one of them found an error in the source document.

The problem

An empty season says every team is identical

Press Baseline on the morning of the first match and the engine has nothing to read. Every metric collapses to its neutral value, and the weighted sum lands on the same number for all seven teams:

win%  0.30 × 0.0 = 0.0    powerplay   0.12 × 0.5 = 6.0 margin 0.04 × 0.5 = 2.0    death overs 0.15 × 0.5 = 7.5 NRR    0.15 × 0.5 = 7.5    schedule    0.02 × 0.0 = 0.0 form   0.12 × 0.5 = 6.0    home/away   0.05 × 0.0 = 0.0                                key players 0.05 × 1.0 = 5.0 ∑ = 34.0  for every team

That is a claim, not a neutral position. It says the reigning champions and the side that won two games all year are the same team. On 7 August they are not — and the evidence that they are not already exists.

Decision 1

Open on last season, not on nothing

The only evidence available before a ball is bowled is what these teams did in 2025. So each side opens on the rating it earned last season, and the table starts differentiated rather than flat. The alternative — rank everyone equal until results arrive — throws away the one thing we know.

ChosenCarry 2025 in as a starting rating.

Decision 2

Derive the opening rating; never type it in

The methodology document commits to one rule above all others: the maths decides the order, the AI does not. A hand-set opening ladder — someone's judgement about who looks strong — would break that rule on the very first graphic of the season.

So the prior is computed, not chosen. The same engine and the same weights are run over the real 2025 season, and whatever that produces is what a team carries in. The useful consequence: retune a weight on the live table and the carry-in moves with it, because both come from one formula.

The 2025 source carries no ball-by-ball data, so powerplay and death overs evaluate to exactly 0.5 for all six teams. They add an identical 13.5 points to every prior — they move the scale, but they cannot change the 2025 order.

ChosenSame engine, same weights, run over 2025.

Decision 3

Count the playoffs

This one changes the top of the table, so it was worth getting right. Trinbago entered the 2025 playoffs third and won three straight to take the title. Judge the season on the league stage alone and the reigning champions open 2026 in fourth.

2025 index — what counts?

The honest counter-argument: only four teams played playoffs, so including them adds matches unevenly — Trinbago are judged on thirteen games and St Kitts on ten. That is a real cost. It was accepted because knockout results are strong evidence about who was actually best, and because a baseline that opens with the champions fourth is not defensible on air.

ChosenAll 34 matches. Cost accepted and stated.

Decision 4

Let last season fade

Last season is evidence, not destiny. Squads change — Saint Lucia lost their captain, their leading spinner and Tim David in one off-season. So the carry-in decays as real 2026 results arrive, and the only question is how fast.

Four was chosen because a CPL side plays only ten league games. Slower, and the graphic is still arguing about last season during the playoffs; faster, and the baseline stops meaning anything after a single upset. At k = 4 half the claim is gone after four matches and roughly a quarter survives to the end of the league stage.

Chosenk = 4, tunable in weights.config.json.

Decision 5

Jamaica cannot be measured, so say so

The Kingsmen are new for 2026. There is no 2025 record to inherit and no honest way to compute one. Three options were on the table: open them at the league average and make no claim; derive a rating from the squad they signed; or apply a stated first-year discount.

Squad-derived was rejected outright — it is a judgement call, and it would put a human thumb on the scale in exactly the place Decision 2 forbids. The discount won because it is arithmetic with a number attached that anyone can argue with: open them σ standard deviations below the 2025 league mean.

Decision 6

A rebrand is not a new team

Barbados Royals became the Barbados Tridents in May 2026, but the squad carried over largely intact. Treating them as an expansion side would have thrown away a full season of real evidence over a change of name, so the franchise inherits the Royals' 2025 record through a priorFrom pointer. Jamaica get the discount; Barbados do not.

Still outstanding. The data file carries the Tridents' name but the Royals' pink-and-navy palette and crest. Inventing a palette would be fabrication, so the licensed artwork needs dropping in before air — until then that row renders as a plain badge.

ChosenInherit the predecessor's record.

Decision 7

Trust last season less when the squad has gone

A record describes the squad that produced it — and these squads changed a great deal over the winter. Twenty-three players switched franchise, thirty-three left the league, and it fell unevenly: Saint Lucia kept barely a third of their side, Barbados two thirds.

There was a tempting shortcut here. The engine already has a key-player term worth 5%, so it looked like the place to put this. It is not: that term is a ratio of available stars to squad stars, so at full strength it reads 1.0 for everyone. Setting Saint Lucia to one star and St Kitts to five changes the opening table by nothing at all. It measures who is injured tonight, not how good the squad is.

So continuity acts on the prior directly: the more of a squad is gone, the closer that team is held to the league average.

Squad continuity — how much is still 2025's team?

TeamKeptLostContinuity 2025 indexAdjustedRating

This measures uncertainty, not blame — and the direction follows from that. Heavy churn makes a bad record less predictive too, so a weak side that rebuilt also drifts toward the middle. Saint Lucia, the most disrupted squad of the seven, therefore finish slightly higher despite losing their captain and their leading spinner. Making churn actively cost a team would mean deciding what Shamsi's seventeen wickets were worth against Noor Ahmad's arrival — and that is opinion, which Decision 2 keeps out of the order.

ChosenContinuity shrinks the prior; floor 0.5.

Verification

The reconstruction reproduces the published table exactly

None of this is worth anything if the 2025 data is wrong. Every match in the season review was re-derived into innings-level records, then the league table was rebuilt from those records and checked against the published one — including net run rate under the ICC full-quota rule.

All six reproduce to three decimal places — but only after a correction. As printed, match 15 made Saint Lucia 4–4 and St Kitts 5–5, contradicting the final table in the same document.

Match 15, 28 August, Daren Sammy Stadium. The review prints “St Kitts & Nevis Patriots 180/3 (17) beat Saint Lucia Kings (H) 177/3”. The two team names are transposed. With them the right way round, all six teams reconcile exactly — every other row was already correct. The source document has been corrected and the correction recorded in it.

Limits

What this baseline does not know

  • The carry-in changes published order by design. That is its purpose, not a side effect. Fresh Start turns it off and ranks on 2026 alone.
  • Two of nine metrics are inert in the prior. No ball-by-ball 2025 source was available, so powerplay and death overs contribute a constant. Re-derive when one exists.
  • k and σ are editorial. They are reasoned and published so they can be argued with, but no live data yet proves these particular values.
  • Continuity counts heads, not quality. Losing a leading wicket-taker weighs the same as losing a fringe seamer. Weighting by contribution needs player valuations, which is the judgement Decision 2 keeps out.
  • The key-player term still does nothing on opening night. It is a ratio of available to squad stars, so it reads 1.0 for everyone until a match reports somebody missing. The squadStars figures in the data file change no opening table.
