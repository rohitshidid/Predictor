Trinbago start on top, and the gap behind them is thinner than it looks

Seven teams, a returning franchise, a rebrand, and a champion who finished third. Where every side starts the 2026 Caribbean Premier League — and what the numbers project once you account for who actually left.

Champhunt · Willow SportsBefore round one

The Caribbean Premier League returns on 7 August with seven teams for the first time, a Jamaica franchise back after seven years away, and a Barbados side playing under a name it last used in 2020. Nobody has bowled a ball. So what follows is a projection, not a table — and the two are not the same thing.

Trinbago Knight Riders start first, which is less obvious than it sounds. They finished third in last year’s league stage, separated from top spot by net run rate alone, then won three straight knockouts for a fifth title. A league table would have them behind Saint Lucia and Guyana. A power ranking has them in front, because winning the tournament is evidence about a side, and finishing third on run rate is mostly evidence about arithmetic.

The sharpest fall belongs to Saint Lucia. They topped the 2025 table with the best net run rate in the league and start fifth, having lost their captain David Wiese, their leading spinner Tabraiz Shamsi and Tim David in a single off-season. Noor Ahmad, Maheesh Theekshana and Charith Asalanka are good replacements. They are not the same team, and the ranking says so.

At the other end, Jamaica are the one entry that is an assumption rather than a measurement. There is no 2025 record to inherit, so the Kingsmen open a stated distance below the league average rather than on anybody’s hunch — and with Rovman Powell captaining a squad that took eight of the off-season’s twenty-three moves, sixth may not last.

Team by team

Ten league games each — 35 in the league stage, 39 with the playoffs.

1

Trinbago Knight Riders ▲2

Champions, and they had to do it the hard way — third in the league on net run rate, then three straight in the playoffs. Andre Russell and Keacy Carty have gone to Jamaica, which is a real cost. But this is the same core that has won five titles, and nobody else starts with that.

Projected 6–4 · rating 83.1 · 11 of 18 retained

2

Guyana Amazon Warriors

Runners-up, and the most complete side on paper last year: Shai Hope’s 491 runs and Imran Tahir’s 23 wickets both led the league. They have lost three players to Jamaica and Moeen Ali to Antigua. Nabi and Gurbaz arrive. Still the team most likely to beat Trinbago to it.

Projected 6–4 · rating 81.2 · 8 of 18 retained

3

Antigua & Barbuda Falcons ▲1

Made the playoffs on points and were carried there by results rather than margins — their net run rate was the worst of the top four by some distance. Moeen Ali comes in to captain, with Evin Lewis, Shadab Khan and Kusal Perera. A better squad than last year’s, starting from a shakier base than the table suggested.

Projected 5–5 · rating 79.5 · 8 of 18 retained

4

St Kitts & Nevis Patriots ▲1

Fifth last year and the quietest improvers of the off-season. Obed McCoy, Johnson Charles, Wanindu Hasaranga and Dasun Shanaka all arrive. The churn is enormous — most of last year’s overseas contingent has gone — so the floor is low and the ceiling is the highest outside the top two.

Projected 5–5 · rating 79.4 · 10 of 18 retained

5

Saint Lucia Kings ▼4

The hardest team to place. They topped the 2025 table with the best net run rate in the league, then lost David Wiese, Tabraiz Shamsi and Tim David. Noor Ahmad, Theekshana and Asalanka are good replacements. But you cannot lose your captain and your leading spinner and start where you finished.

Projected 5–5 · rating 79.2 · 7 of 18 retained

6

Jamaica Kingsmen NEW

The expansion side, and the one entry here that is a stated assumption rather than a measurement — there is no 2025 record to inherit, so they open a fixed distance below the league average. Rovman Powell captains, Andre Russell comes home, and eight of the off-season’s 23 moves went to Jamaica. Sixth is a placeholder they may make look silly.

Projected 4–6 · rating 77.6 · expansion side

7

Barbados Tridents ▼1

New name, same problem. Two wins in ten last season was the worst record in the league, and Rovman Powell has left for Jamaica. Motie, Chris Green and George Linde arrive to steady the bowling. The rebrand does not reset the record — they inherit the Royals’ season, because it was the same squad.

Projected 4–6 · rating 77.1 · 12 of 18 retained

The win–loss column is a prediction, not a result. Nothing has been played. Each side’s projected record comes from its opening rating, run against every opponent over the ten league games it plays, and the seven records add up to the 35 league matches actually on the schedule. As soon as real results exist, every graphic switches back to the real record automatically.
